    46.5 feasibility audit: three blockers, and XPert's training path does not apply its attention mask
    
    Before buying an hour of the 5.8 that 66.8 committed, I audited whether their code runs here. It does,
    but not for 5.8 h and not without declared deviations. Zero GPU-hours.
    
    Good news first: their trainer takes --nfold split_cold_cell_1 natively and the 68,830-row subset
    carries split_cold_cell_1..5 as native obs columns -- the same splits v9 was evaluated on. So the
    comparison is their code, their data, their split definition, and head_to_head_mdmt.py already refuses
    to pair unless row_index matches exactly.
    
    Blocker 1: num_epochs 2500 with patience 50. Runtime is early-stopping driven and unbounded. My 5.8 h
    figure was a v9-shaped estimate and does not transfer. Needs a wall-clock guard and a max-epoch cap,
    and the cap is itself a deviation from their recipe.
    
    Blocker 2: flash_attn is a hard training dependency. My first reading was that it is dead code under
    sparse_flag=False by analogy with 47.6 -- WRONG, and I checked before acting. The branch is on
    output_attention, which train_xpert.py defaults to False, so training takes the flash path. 47.6 is
    confirmed from a second angle though: sparse_flag is threaded through four signatures and never
    branched on anywhere.
    
    Blocker 3 is the finding. The flash branch is called with no mask argument, while the drug attention
    mask IS constructed by get_unimol_drug_feat and passed at model_XPert.py:225. So in the training
    configuration the mask is computed, passed, and not applied. Measured on their own data: mean 53.9
    valid atoms of 122 slots, 55.8% of all slots are padding, padded features exactly zero. Padded slots
    are not inert -- key and value are nn.Linear with bias=True, so each emits one identical constant --
    and at equal scores padding takes 55.8% of the softmax mass on average, 95.9% for the smallest
    molecules. All ~68 padded slots share one key, so suppressing them requires beating a count of 68.
    
    68.5 lists what I am NOT claiming, because I have been wrong three times criticising this paper and
    the pattern each time was asserting from code-reading without exhausting alternatives. I cannot verify
    what the RELEASED CHECKPOINT was trained with; I have not run flash_attn directly; and a constant
    padded value could in principle be absorbed harmlessly.
    
    Consequence: "train XPert as published" is now a choice between two different models -- SDPA without
    the mask (faithful to the code) or with it (faithful to the intent) -- and they are not the same
    experiment, because v9 masks padding properly. NOT SPENDING YET. Packet 007 puts the choice and the
    epoch cap to review first.

    Review 006: the operator was masking the wrong thing all along, and 46.5 wins the next 5.8 hours
    
    Six challenges, all six upheld, three verified by me against the code or arithmetic first. Tally 51/54.
    
    C1 is the most consequential finding in six rounds on this question and neither of us caught it until
    now. Verified in code: model_v9.py:139 puts the GLOBAL TOKEN at position 0 of the drug sequence -- it
    carries ECFP4, RDKit descriptors and the Uni-Mol CLS -- and modules_v9.py:244 blends the WHOLE LxL
    matrix. So at alpha=0 the global token cannot read the atoms and no atom can read it. Every masking
    operator used so far removed global-atom flow TOGETHER WITH atom-atom flow, while the hypothesis is
    about atom-atom only. That is very likely where most of the 0.09-0.12 S11-S10 came from, which is why
    every estimand built on it drowned.
    
    So 65.3's "the whole family is confounded" was too strong and is amended: THIS operator is retired, and
    the family has an untried member that matches the hypothesis -- blend only A[..., 1:, 1:]. Free.

    INEQUALITY (null key span below 25% of the hypothesis span, chosen because the two failed gates came in

    below +0.03 the operator is abandoned without computing any interaction.
